The quantification method of wing shape parameters
The wing shape parameters (AR and SMC) were quantified using a multi-step computational
pipeline designed for reproducible feature extraction. First, the wing contour was isolated from
each image using the Segment Anything Model [SAM, (87)] with a Vision Transformer
backbone [ViT-B, (137)]. For comprehensive segmentation, we employed an automatic mask-
generation procedure that identifies and delineates visible structures in the image without manual
prompting. The generation parameters were configured to be inclusive, with a predicted
Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold of 0.8 and a stability-score threshold of 0.9, so that fine-
scale features were retained. From the resulting candidate masks, the one encompassing the
largest pixel area, corresponding to the complete wing silhouette, was selected for subsequent
analysis.
     Following extraction of the binary wing mask, a custom computational routine was used to
calculate the morphological parameters. The total wing area (S) was computed by summing the
non-zero pixels in the selected mask. Wing span (b) was determined by fitting a minimum-area
bounding rectangle to the wing contour; the length of the major axis of this rectangle was taken
as the span. This workflow provides a consistent high-throughput approach for quantifying wing
morphology across the dataset.

Region-level concordance of HAIKA attention
To avoid treating attention maps as causal explanations, we used them only as qualitative
plausibility checks and added a region-level concordance assessment. In 210 Hymenoptera
images, the dominant HAIKA attention region included the expert-recognized midwing region
(Fig. S8) in 203 cases (96.7%). This result indicates that the attention pattern usually overlaps
with an anatomically informative wing region, but it does not establish formal homology or
causal interpretability.

Repeatability and embedding stability
To assess stability across model initialization, HAIKA training and evaluation were repeated
across five independent random seeds. The resulting clustering metrics remained stable across
PCA, t-SNE, and k-means evaluations (table S9). Cross-seed comparisons of embedding
geometry and clade-cohesion structure also showed high consistency (table S10). These analyses
reduce the likelihood that the reported benchmark results reflect a single favorable initialization.

Fig. S7. Comparison of evolutionary rates between morphological trait axes PC1 and PC2.
The histogram displays the bootstrap distribution of the log-transformed ratio of evolutionary
rates for PC1 versus PC2 based on 1000 simulations. This distribution represents the null
hypothesis that the rates are equal (log-ratio = 0). The solid red vertical line indicates the
observed log (rate ratio), corresponding to an untransformed ratio of 1.698. The 95% confidence
interval of the null distribution is [0.735, 1.364]. The observed ratio falls outside this interval (P
= 0.001), indicating that the estimated evolutionary rate of PC1 is greater than that of PC2 under
the fitted model.
