HAIKA and ontophylo wing rate comparison

Objective
Compare where and when wing-associated morphological evolution is relatively
fast in the ontophylo reconstruction and in the continuous HAIKA image traits.
The fixed reference is the study's dated 77-tip topology. Raw rates have
different units and are not estimates of the same anatomical character set.

1. Establish the actual inputs
Freeze source hashes and repository commits. Extract released Case 3 features
and published PC1/PC2, check their agreement, and record the discrepancy between
the paper's stated 64-dimensional representation and the released workbook.
Audit the 77 molecular exemplars, morphological exemplars, and HAIKA labels
separately. Do not silently substitute taxa, copy family means to tips, or infer
missing image traits from ontophylo rates.

2. Define comparable samples
Use exact molecular-species overlap as a strict sensitivity. Use justified
same-genus image exemplars as an explicitly labeled broader comparison where
necessary. Where HAIKA coverage is incomplete, fit on the induced subtree of
the 77-tip tree; do not describe prior-only rates at unobserved tips as inferred
image evidence. Export every taxon assignment and exclusion. Preserve original
node ages when branches are contracted.

3. Reconstruct continuous rates on Roihu
Start with the published PC1 and PC2 separately, preserving their biological
interpretation and avoiding an assumption that all latent dimensions are
independent traits. Fit homogeneous BM and phytools multirateBM penalized ML
models with a prespecified penalty grid (0.1, 1, 10). Record convergence and
optimization sensitivity; do not choose the penalty using correlation with
ontophylo or ordinary AIC with the nominal parameter count. Extract the
likelihood's logarithmic mean of parent and child node rates for each branch.
Joint modeling or a feature-based PCA sensitivity depends on the data audit;
it must not be presented as equivalent to separately fitted axes.

4. Put both reconstructions on the same branches
Primary ontophylo comparison: fore-wing transition counts / Myr, with the
cuticular-only subset as an anatomical sensitivity. All-wings and hind-wing
rates are secondary complementary comparisons. Sum original map counts along
each retained path before dividing by its total duration. Keep all 1,000
existing map replicates. Document that these onto histories condition on the
full original sample; assess matched-tip refitting when feasible.

5. Compare rates and marginalize through time
Compare branch ranks, log-relative rates where defined, and branch categories.
Normalize each realization by its tree-length-weighted mean, separately for
each representation, so the common scale is relative evolutionary activity.
Marginal rates average over lineages alive at each time. For finite bins use
rate times overlapping branch duration divided by total lineage exposure.
Use the same branches, times, weights and bins for HAIKA and ontophylo. Show
sampling coverage alongside trajectories. Exclude or separately report the
two basal edges whose individual BM variances are weakly identifiable.

6. Quantify limits and robustness
Ontophylo map intervals condition on the fitted discrete model; they are not
BM uncertainty. For HAIKA, use appropriate simulation/refitting uncertainty
and penalty sensitivity. Calibrate branch association against homogeneous-BM
tip simulations refitted through the same pipeline on the same tree; naive
branch shuffling or treating time bins as independent replicates is inadequate.
Report the null tested and Monte Carlo resolution. Assess exact versus proxy
taxon matching, anatomy partition, PC axis and penalty. Avoid interpreting
agreement as validation of character homology or disagreement as method error.

Deliverables
Auditable source and taxon tables; readable scripts and Roihu job manifests;
branch estimates and marginal curves; editable scientific figures; a numerical
report with convergence, sensitivity, uncertainty and interpretive limits.

Resolved alignment and analysis scope
The user explicitly authorized family-level image proxies as sensitivity and
then requested alignment as close as possible to the original 77-tip tree.
All 77 tips can be aligned using literal source-image family labels, covering
48 historical morphology families and 112 images. Forty-one families have
only one source image. No species identity is inferred from those labels.
The main requested 77-tip comparison is therefore explicitly conditional on
family proxies; it is not a species-resolved empirical comparison.

The published Case 3 species matrix has cross-taxon profile inconsistencies,
and its published PC scores cannot be reproduced from the released raw matrix.
It is excluded from the primary inference. Genus labels in the S4 matrix also
replicate family templates and do not solve this problem. Use original
image-labelled matrices, preserving all source assignments instead.

The primary input is the archived 64-dimensional embedding from seed 3407.
Compute family means, then center (without unit-variance rescaling) and fit PCA
to the 48 family means with equal family weight. Retain at least two axes and
enough to explain 90% of variance (three axes, 96.94%, for seed 3407). Assign
these family scores to the existing 77 tip positions. These new PC axes must
not be called the published wing-shape and venation axes.

Fit each retained axis separately. Sum raw axis diffusivities as an exploratory
total variance rate before relative normalization; this sum does not assume
independent uncertainty between fitted axes. Compare all77, two deterministic
48-family representative subsets, four additional ANN seeds, and released28D
inputs. The 51-fit manifest is fixed before inspecting rate associations.

Interpret within-family slow rates in the full77 analysis as potentially imposed
by duplicated profiles. Report interfamily branches and the48-family analyses
separately. Three historical morphology families are nonmonophyletic on this
tree, so representative selection is itself a sensitivity.

For the conditional association null, estimate a homogeneous multivariate BM
covariance on the48-family representative tree, retaining cross-PC covariance.
Simulate family profiles on that tree and duplicate each profile to the same77
tip assignments before refitting. Also refit the48-family tree. This tests
association beyond shared phylogenetic geometry and family-proxy duplication,
conditional on the fixed PCA, estimated null covariance and selected tree.
Use199 simulations (one-tail probability grid0.005; doubled-minimum-tail
grid0.010); no naive branch shuffling.

Additional numerical diagnostic
Boundary behavior in the completed primary fits prompted 18 additional fits:
three PCs on the 77-tip and first 48-family trees, with lower log-rate offsets
5 and 15 (versus the original 10), and a strong-smoothing penalty equal to the
original root age. This diagnostic was selected before inspecting its own
associations, but after examining the primary numerical behavior. It is not
part of the original 51-fit manifest. See results/bound-sensitivity.csv.
The existing 199-replicate null applies only to lambda 1, not to the strong
smoothing diagnostic. Sensitivity figures distinguish these configurations.
