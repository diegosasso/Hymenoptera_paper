The user supplied the S3 matrix workbook, S4 phylogenetic archive, supplement PDF,
and supplementary tables. S1 code and S8 independent-seed embeddings were retrieved

What the features measure
The ANN is a self-supervised residual channel-attention autoencoder. Inputs were
standardized forewing line drawings, rather than unprocessed specimen photographs:
orthogonal views, manual tracing/refinement, orientation along the long axis of a
B5 canvas, grayscale 300 dpi output. Primary venation was emphasized; chromatic
and spatial distribution features were excluded (main-paper Methods). Training
images were resized to 128 by 128 and normalized to [0,1]. The paper specifies a
64-dimensional latent vector, and archived main.py sets LATENT_DIM = 64.

ANN values are continuous image embeddings. They are not discrete homologous
characters or independent anatomical traits. The supplement explicitly states
that attention overlap does not establish homology or causal interpretability.
The published image archive contains forewings, so the closest ontophylo anatomical
comparison is forewing; total wings and hindwing are secondary comparisons.

The source S3 workbook has no feature-column names. Extraction assigns positional
names feature_01 through feature_28 without claiming anatomical identities.
There are no missing or constant columns in its three feature matrices. S4 has
12 constant columns. The S8 seeds have respectively 33, 23, 36, 28, and 39 constant
columns. Constant columns must be removed before variance-standardized PCA. Each
seed describes its own learned coordinates; corresponding feature indices should
not be equated across seeds.

S3's 28 dimensions versus the stated 64 remains unexplained. Seed91507 has 28
variable dimensions, but those values do not exactly reproduce the S3 matrix after
taxon matching and dropping constant columns. This is not evidence that a specific
transformation generated the released S3 data. S8 provides direct 64D embeddings
and is suitable for an explicitly new analysis with independent seed sensitivity.

The archived ANN script saves feature values rounded to three decimal places.
Exact vector identity therefore refers to the published rounded values, not proof
that the underlying full-precision network outputs or image files were identical.

Published PC-axis and species provenance
The paper assigns 42.5% and 32.0% variance to PC1/PC2 and interprets these axes in
relation to shape and venation. It fits global BM, OU and EB models to PC scores,
then uses fastAnc and absolute node-to-parent differences for maps. Those node
differences are not branch-specific diffusion-rate estimates.

The S5 PC scores were extracted directly, preserving original species labels and
source rows. Their taxon set exactly matches the 169 Case3 rows. However, ordinary
PCA on the released Case3 feature matrix does not reproduce those published axes:
  Center only: first two variance fractions 0.491919 and 0.391587;
    correlations with published PC1/PC2 0.429342 and -0.462777.
  Center and unit variance: fractions 0.440270 and 0.316624;
    correlations 0.609630 and -0.588368.
Signs of PCA axes are arbitrary. These diagnostics do not rule out other
preprocessing, but the transformation/provenance is currently unresolved.

Named species rows are not proof of independent species-level measurements.
Among 169 Case3 vectors, 167 exactly match at least one Case2 vector. Of these,
162 match one source row and five match two rows. Some matches merit clarification:
  Tetragonula_carbonaria matches Apocrita-Trigonalidae in all 28 columns.
  Osmia_cornuta and Heriades_truncorum match Aculeata-Osirini_Osiris.
  Xylocopa_violacea and Megachile_willughbiella share the same vector, which occurs
    in both Aculeata-Xylocopini_Xylocopa and Aculeata-Megachile_Megachile.
This is a factual equality audit, not a reconstruction of the authors' intent.
There is insufficient metadata to determine specimen identities or the cause of
these assignments. Published PC1/PC2 pairs are repeated across 169 labels with
only 71 distinct pairs, including a group of 14 sphecid/crabronid species.
The Case3 scores should not be the primary species-level validation dataset.

Taxon matching to the study tree
The study's tree-tip-metadata.csv preserves both molecular species/genus labels
and morphology exemplars. These sometimes differ and must not be conflated.
Direct Case2 image-label matching finds zero morphology exemplar genera and only
one proper molecular genus (Elasmus). A molecular field labeled Megalyridae is a
family placeholder, not a second genus match. S4 can give 16 morphology-genus
matches (mostly sawflies), but repeated higher-taxon profiles limit independence.

The full S2 raw-image ZIP directory was inspected via HTTP Range, transferring
only 155 KB rather than the full 565 MB. Its 1140 entries are images/directories;
there are no metadata tables. Image names retain the same broad taxonomic labels,
so the archive does not resolve the missing species identities. The filenames
are preserved in provenance/haika-image-archive-filenames.txt.

Authorized 77-tip family-proxy alignment
Following the user's express authorization for a close 77-tip alignment using
family-level proxies, haika-family-proxy-crosswalk.csv selects source images by
exact Family_Sharkey tokens appearing in original Case2 image labels. It matches
all 77 tips to 48 named families and 112 distinct source images. No family was
inferred from a genus/subfamily/tribe, and no synonym replacement was required.
The long image list is haika-family-proxy-source-rows.csv. Family means should be
computed from these explicit source rows, retaining the conditional interpretation.

Forty-one of the 48 families have only one source image; three have two, one four,
one six, one 26, and one 29. Consequently image-resampling sensitivity cannot
quantify missing within-family variation for most families.

The alternative molecular-family field matches 74 tips. Four family names differ:
  Megastigmus: Torymidae (morphology) versus Megastigmidae (molecular; no image).
  Ismarus: Diapriidae versus Ismaridae (no image).
  Archaeoteleia: Platygastridae versus Sparasionidae (no image).
  Telenomus/Trissolcus: Platygastridae versus Scelionidae (both represented).
Both alternatives are recorded, so the historical family classification used for
the morphology analysis is explicit rather than silently substituted.

The 77-tip result is a conditional projection of family wing profiles onto the
study tree. Several tips inherit exactly the same family aggregate. Such repeated
inputs can artificially suppress inferred rates within families and accentuate
between-family changes. A one-representative-per-family (48-tip) comparison and
seed sensitivity are therefore required for interpretation. Neither 77 nor 48 is
the number of independently observed species from the HAIKA image collection.

S3/S8 label harmonization
All 405 S8 labels align one-to-one with S3 after two fully recorded label rules:
  Apocrita_nn_Aculeata- prefix in S8 corresponds to Apocrita- in S3.
  S8 Aculeata-Sphecidae Ammophilinae corresponds to the literal S3 spelling
    Aculeata-Sphecidae Arnmophilinae.
Both original strings are retained in haika-case2-seed-label-crosswalk.csv.

Reproduction
Run from repository root with Python, openpyxl and numpy:
  python comparisons/haika-wings/scripts/01-extract-haika.py
  python comparisons/haika-wings/scripts/01-extract-haika-family-crosswalk.py
These scripts perform lightweight extraction and provenance diagnostics only.
No ANN was trained and no evolutionary model was fitted by these scripts.

Independent review of prepared analysis inputs (2026-11-20)
Reviewed scripts/03-prepare-comparison.R, data/prepared-datasets.rds,
data/family-pca-models.rds, all exported trait tables, and the fit manifest.
No blocking input or join error was found. Independently recalculating the family
means from the original source-label lists reproduced every saved family matrix.
The scripts count each distinct image label once per family, regardless of the
number of study tips receiving that family profile. New PCA is fitted to the
48 family means, so families do not receive extra weight merely from having more
study tips. Covariance PCA uses centering without unit-variance standardization.

All 13 prepared datasets have finite data, matching tree/trait orders, and
48 unique profiles. The full77 datasets repeat those 48 profiles at 77 tips.
The family48_first and family48_last datasets each retain one tip per family.
All pairwise phylogenetic distances among retained tips exactly match the source
tree to numerical tolerance, and every dataset retains root age 280.02588667 Ma.
Saved PC scores reproduce the PCA projection of the independent family means
to less than 1e-10. No source images or family labels were silently substituted.
The existing crosswalk correctly preserves the four morphology/molecular-family
differences described above.

Proxy duplication and branch flags
Nineteen families occur at multiple study tips, accounting for 48 of the 77 tips.
There are 29 extra assignments beyond one per family. At the branch level:
  152 original branches are represented in haika-branch-proxy-flags.csv.
  102 branches lead to descendants from only one family (including all terminals).
  50 branches genuinely lie within a single-family subtree: both their parent
    and child subtend only that same family. Their descendant input profiles are
    identical and contain no measured within-family phenotypic contrast.

Field definitions in haika-branch-proxy-flags.csv:
  nfamilies: distinct assigned families among a branch's descendant tips.
  descendants_single_family: nfamilies equals one.
  within_single_family: both parent and child descendants contain one family.
  n_study_tips_assigned_same_family: global multiplicity of that assigned family,
    supplied only for branches whose descendants contain a single family.
  n_repeated_assignments_in_descendants: descendant tip count minus family count.
The distinction matters: a stem leading to a single-family subtree can represent
a between-family contrast; it should not automatically be labeled a within-family
branch just because all its descendants belong to one family.

Three repeatedly assigned families are nonmonophyletic on the study tree:
Pteromalidae (Cleonymus, Nasonia), Cynipidae (Periclistus, Diplolepis), and Figitidae
(Melanips, Anacharis, Parnips). The 48-tip subsets are representative selections,
not a simple collapse of 48 monophyletic clades. This is an additional reason to
retain both first/last representative sensitivity results and to interpret the
77-tip fit as a conditional family-profile projection.
