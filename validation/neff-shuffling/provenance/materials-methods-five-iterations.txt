\subsection*{Testing \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\)}
We evaluated the performance and robustness of \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) using recovery simulations, sensitivity analyses and a randomization test. The recovery study included 100 trials in each of two scenarios designed to generate correlated or independent phenomic and lineage diversification. Reconstructed \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) showed significant positive Pearson correlations with diversification in 78 of 100 Correlated trials. No significant correlations were detected in the 100 Independent trials at the nominal \(p<0.05\) threshold. This result describes detection under the simulated conditions, rather than proving absence of an association.

We tested sensitivity to the branch-rate percentile used to identify accelerated branches by recalculating the metric at the 92.5th, 95th and 97.5th percentiles. Correlations with PyRate net diversification remained positive and nominally significant across these thresholds (Supplementary Note 1). We also evaluated the frequency component \(f^{+}\) and phenomic-breadth component \(H^{+}\) using additive and interaction models for \(\log(f^{+})\) and \(H^{+}\). The interaction model received greater support (Supplementary Note 2).

Because temporal changes in branch availability could affect the distribution of acceleration patterns, we randomly reassigned accelerated branches independently within each of 15 anatomical units on the empirical 77-tip phylogeny. We retained the tree and per-unit accelerated-branch counts and generated 9,999 randomizations. For each randomization, we recalculated \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) across the 1,000 reconstruction samples and correlated the resulting mean trajectory with fixed PyRate net diversification in thirteen 20-Myr bins. None of the randomizations produced a significant positive correlation or reached the observed correlation of \(r=0.950\), yielding a one-sided permutation \(p=0.0001\).

\subsection*{Testing \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\)}
We assessed recovery of the \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\)--diversification association using 100 paired trials in Correlated and Independent character-evolution scenarios (Supplementary Method 9). After reconstructing \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) from simulated tip states, we calculated two-sided Pearson correlations with generating diversification rates. Significant positive associations were detected in 78/100 Correlated trials, whereas no significant associations were detected in 100 Independent trials (nominal \(p<0.05\)).

We recalculated \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) using the 92.5th, 95th and 97.5th branch-rate percentiles to identify accelerated branches. Its association with PyRate net diversification remained positive and significant at all three thresholds (Supplementary Note 1). We examined the contributions of acceleration frequency \(f^{+}\) and phenomic breadth \(H^{+}\) by comparing models with additive and interacting effects of \(\log(f^{+})\) and \(H^{+}\); the interaction model was better supported (Supplementary Note 2).

To test whether branch availability could generate the empirical association, we performed 9,999 unrestricted randomizations of accelerated-branch assignments within the 15 anatomical units on the fixed 77-tip phylogeny. Each unit's complete 1,000-sample branch profiles were permuted together, preserving its accelerated-branch count in every sample. We recalculated the mean \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) trajectory and its correlation with fixed PyRate net diversification across thirteen 20-Myr bins. None of the randomizations yielded a significant positive association or equalled the observed \(r=0.950\) (one-sided permutation \(p=0.0001\)).

We evaluated \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) for association recovery, threshold sensitivity, component contributions and effects of branch availability. Recovery was tested using 100 paired trials in Correlated and Independent scenarios (Supplementary Method 9). Component analyses supported an interaction between \(\log(f^{+})\) and \(H^{+}\) over additive effects, linking the association to both the frequency and phenomic breadth of accelerated evolution (Supplementary Note 2).
