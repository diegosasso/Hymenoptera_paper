1. Use the exact four scenarios and binary ordering from Extended Data Figure 4
\subsection*{Calculating \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) and \(P_c\): a worked example}
The example has three lineages and three anatomical units, ordered as wings, thorax and head. Accelerated units are coded 1; other units are coded 0. Scenario 1 has patterns 111, 111, 111; Scenario 2 has 100, 100, 100; Scenario 3 has 001, 010, 100; Scenario 4 has 000, 000, 000.

Neff+ is the proportion of lineages with at least one accelerated unit multiplied by the exponential of the Shannon entropy of the nonzero patterns. Scenario 3 has three equally frequent patterns, so its entropy is ln(3) and Neff+ is 3. Scenarios 1 and 2 have only one pattern each, so their entropy is zero and Neff+ is 1. Scenario 4 has no acceleration and Neff+ is zero.

Pc averages the fraction of accelerated units on each lineage. It is 1 in Scenario 1, 1/3 in Scenarios 2 and 3, and 0 in Scenario 4. Therefore, Pc must be calculated from the binary patterns, not from the Neff+ value alone.

2. Explain what is counted before introducing the formula
\subsection*{Calculating \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) and \(P_c\): a worked example}
Consider a time slice crossing the three terminal branches in each scenario of Extended Data Figure 4. Each lineage is represented by a three-digit pattern in the order wings, thorax, head. A 1 means accelerated and a 0 means not accelerated. The entire combination defines a pattern: 111 is one pattern even though all three units are accelerated.

Let N be the number of lineages, n+ the number with at least one 1, and p_i the frequency of each distinct pattern among those n+ lineages. Calculate f+ = n+/N, H+ = -sum(p_i ln p_i), and Neff+ = f+ exp(H+). In Scenario 3, n+ = N = 3 and every pattern has frequency 1/3. Thus f+ = 1, H+ = ln(3), and Neff+ = 3. If no lineage is accelerated, define Neff+ = 0 without evaluating the entropy.

Pc uses the same binary data. Count the accelerated units in each row, divide by three, and average over all three rows. Scenario 3 gives Pc = (1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3)/3 = 1/3. Include rows of zeros in this average.

\subsection*{Calculating \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) and \(P_c\): a worked example}
Encode each lineage using three binary entries for wings, thorax and head. The whole combination is a single pattern. For each time slice, calculate the fraction of lineages with at least one accelerated unit, f+, and the Shannon entropy H+ of the nonzero pattern frequencies; Neff+ = f+ exp(H+).

In Scenario 3, the patterns 001, 010 and 100 each occur once. All three lineages are accelerated, so f+ = 1; the equally frequent patterns give H+ = -3(1/3)ln(1/3) = ln(3). Therefore Neff+ = 3.

For Pc, divide the number of accelerated units on each lineage by the total number of units, then average across all lineages. Scenario 3 gives Pc = (1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3)/3 = 1/3.

Scenario | Patterns | f+ | H+ | Neff+ | Pc
1 | 111, 111, 111 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1
2 | 100, 100, 100 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1/3
3 | 001, 010, 100 | 1 | ln(3) | 3 | 1/3
4 | 000, 000, 000 | 0 | not evaluated | 0 | 0

Scenario 1 has the greatest anatomical extent of acceleration, whereas Scenario 3 has the greatest diversity of patterns across lineages. Scenarios 1 and 2 demonstrate that identical Neff+ values can correspond to different Pc values.

4. Explain zero patterns and the weighting factor with an explicitly modified example
\subsection*{Calculating \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) and \(P_c\): a worked example}
Use the three lineages and three anatomical units in Extended Data Figure 4. The binary order is wings, thorax, head. A pattern is the complete combination of accelerated units on one lineage, so 111 counts as one pattern.

Calculate f+ as the fraction of lineages containing at least one 1. Calculate H+ from the frequencies of distinct nonzero patterns among those lineages, using natural logarithms. Then Neff+ = f+ exp(H+). In Scenario 3, f+ = 1 and p_i = 1/3 for each of the three patterns, giving H+ = ln(3) and Neff+ = 3. If there are no nonzero patterns, set Neff+ = 0.

Calculate Pc by averaging the fraction of accelerated units on each lineage, including lineages coded 000. With three units and three lineages, Scenario 3 gives Pc = (1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3)/3 = 1/3. The four scenarios give (Neff+, Pc) values of (1,1), (1,1/3), (3,1/3) and (0,0).

As an additional illustration, replace the third pattern in Scenario 3 with 000. The remaining two patterns each have frequency 1/2 among accelerated lineages: f+ = 2/3, H+ = ln(2), Neff+ = 4/3 and Pc = 2/9. Thus zero patterns are omitted from H+ but retained when determining the denominators of f+ and Pc.

5. Final concise worked example, with both metrics and all four figure scenarios
\subsection*{Calculating \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) and \(P_c\): a worked example}
Consider a time slice crossing the three terminal branches in each scenario of Extended Data Figure~4. Code each lineage in the order \emph{wings, thorax, head}, with 1 for accelerated and 0 otherwise. Each complete combination is one pattern: \texttt{111} is a single pattern even though all three units accelerate.

\paragraph{1. Calculate \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\).}
Let \(N\) be the number of lineages and \(n^{+}\) the number containing at least one 1. Among these \(n^{+}\) lineages, calculate the frequency \(p_i\) of each distinct pattern. Using natural logarithms,
\[
f^{+}=\frac{n^{+}}{N},\qquad
H^{+}=-\sum_i p_i\ln p_i,\qquad
N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}=f^{+}e^{H^{+}}.
\]
In Scenario~3, the patterns \texttt{001}, \texttt{010} and \texttt{100} each occur once. Thus \(f^{+}=3/3=1\) and each \(p_i=1/3\), giving
\[
H^{+}=-3\left(\frac13\ln\frac13\right)=\ln3,
\qquad N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}=1\times e^{\ln3}=3.
\]
If no lineage is accelerated (Scenario~4), set \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}=0\) without evaluating \(H^{+}\).

\paragraph{2. Calculate phenomic coordination, \(P_c\).}
For each lineage, divide its number of accelerated units, \(a_j\), by the total number of units, \(L\), then average across \emph{all} lineages. In Scenario~3, each lineage has one accelerated unit out of three:
\[
P_c=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{a_j}{L}
=\frac{1/3+1/3+1/3}{3}=\frac13.
\]

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{clcccc}
\toprule
Scenario & Lineage patterns & \(f^{+}\) & \(H^{+}\) & \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) & \(P_c\) \\
\midrule
1 & \texttt{111, 111, 111} & 1 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\
2 & \texttt{100, 100, 100} & 1 & 0 & 1 & \(1/3\) \\
3 & \texttt{001, 010, 100} & 1 & \(\ln3\) & 3 & \(1/3\) \\
4 & \texttt{000, 000, 000} & 0 & --- & 0 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Scenario~1 has the highest \(P_c\) because all units accelerate on every lineage. Scenario~3 has the highest \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) because different units accelerate on different lineages. The two metrics therefore describe different properties; \(P_c\) cannot be obtained from the \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) value alone.

To illustrate the effect of an unaccelerated lineage, replace \texttt{100} in Scenario~3 with \texttt{000}. Then \(f^{+}=2/3\), the two remaining patterns each have frequency \(1/2\), and \(H^{+}=\ln2\), giving \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}=4/3\) and \(P_c=2/9\). The zero pattern is excluded from \(H^{+}\), but its lineage remains in the denominators of \(f^{+}\) and \(P_c\).
