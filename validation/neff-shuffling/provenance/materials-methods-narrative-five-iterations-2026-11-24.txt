\subsection*{Testing \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\)}
To assess the robustness of the inferred correlation between \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) and PyRate net diversification rates, we ran a series of analyses.

First, we assessed recovery of the association using 100 paired trials in Correlated and Independent scenarios (Supplementary Method 9). In the Correlated scenario, character evolution was linked to diversification; in the Independent scenario, it was not. Reconstructed \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) yielded significant positive Pearson correlations with simulated diversification rates in 78/100 Correlated trials. No significant correlations were detected in the 100 Independent trials (nominal two-sided \(p<0.05\)). These results support the ability of the method to recover the expected association under the simulated conditions.

We assessed sensitivity of \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) to the thresholds used to identify accelerated branches. For this, we recalculated \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) using the 92.5th, 95th and 97.5th branch-rate percentiles. Its association with PyRate net diversification remained positive and significant across these thresholds, indicating that the result did not depend on one particular cutoff (Supplementary Note 1).

We also tested the relative contributions of the two components of \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\), \(f^{+}\) and \(H^{+}\), to the correlation. These components describe the frequency of accelerated evolution across lineages and the diversity of the anatomical acceleration patterns involved. Both contributed to the association, and their interaction improved model fit (Supplementary Note 2).

Finally, \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) is calculated from the distribution of accelerated patterns across branches, while lineage diversification changes the branching pattern. Periods with more branches could therefore provide more opportunities to observe distinct acceleration patterns, generating part of the correlation through tree structure alone. To test this, we independently shuffled accelerated-branch assignments within 15 anatomical units on the fixed 77-tip phylogeny, preserving the number of accelerated branches in each unit and reconstruction sample. For each of 9,999 randomizations, we recalculated \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}\) across 1,000 samples, averaged the trajectories and correlated the mean with unchanged PyRate net diversification rates across thirteen 20-Myr bins. None produced a significant positive correlation or reached the observed \(r=0.950\) (one-sided permutation \(p=0.0001\)). Random placement of accelerated branches therefore did not reproduce the observed positive association.
