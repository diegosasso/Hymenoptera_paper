\subsection*{Recovery and specificity of accelerated phenomic diversity}
\phantomsection\label{sec:paired-neff-main}
We evaluated recovery of accelerated phenomic diversity
($N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}$) using 100 paired simulations. Each pair shared a
300-Myr pure-birth tree with a 100-Myr interval of elevated speciation.
In the associated scenario, ten contemporaneous branches within that interval
represented ten anatomical units, each with 20 binary characters originating
on its focal branch and evolving slowly thereafter. In the unassociated
control, all 200 characters evolved independently from root state~0 under a
homogeneous equal-rates model throughout the same tree, without focal origins
or dependence on the diversification regime.

Using only each tree and its terminal character states, we fitted ER models
and reconstructed 100 conditional histories per variable character with
\texttt{ontophylo}. We compared $N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{+}$ calculated from the
reconstructed histories with the same metric calculated from exact simulated
histories. The primary analysis classified branch rates strictly above each
unit's 95th percentile as accelerated. We correlated diversity with known
generating diversification in 15 matched 20-Myr bins and reported the
fractions meeting nominal two-sided thresholds, both irrespective of sign
and requiring a positive correlation. The control estimates false-association
frequencies under this specified independent-character process; the Pearson
tests themselves do not correct for temporal autocorrelation. Full design,
conditioning, reconstruction and metric definitions are given in
\hyperref[sec:paired-neff-supplement]{Supplementary Methods}.
